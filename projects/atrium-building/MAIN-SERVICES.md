## Inspection

Expand **Main service routes · R01**, then choose **Service route plan** and a floor. Each of the 14 systems has its own checkbox/color. The master **Concept services** switch hides all systems. The equipment panel's **R01 service routes** switch mirrors that master, and its earlier P01 connection studies are suppressed to prevent duplicate routes.

Compact FR/WL/WR/AHU/dry-riser labels identify the route plan; extinguisher models remain while their unrelated plan labels are suppressed in this inspection mode. The route/source/shaft selector focuses a candidate and identifies its PDF page and conceptual state. Floors, roofs, ceilings, room selection and fullscreen controls continue to work together. Hidden parent groups are excluded from picking. Access/penetration volumes and service-insulation envelopes are separate optional overlays and do not intercept selection. Shaft/plant volumes can be hidden independently. Fire-alarm and atrium-smoke reservations start hidden.

The model contains 379 route records after D02's five additional illustrative room-supply branches, including examples and pending interfaces, 43 reservation volumes, three floor-control assemblies, 12 sample indoor cooling units and one ground ventilation reservation. Counts are a modeling inventory, not calculated equipment requirements or system completeness.

## Coordination and assumptions

- The older roof-fed supply/return drawings and roof AHU alternatives are superseded and hidden; their catalog IDs remain in the model. Solid roofs, screens, roof access and sanitary discharges remain. The old west exterior chase shell is retained without claiming it is a current fresh-air source. New ventilation follows R01 floor AHUs and the yard unit.
- Upper AHU air ports now face upward, leaving west maintenance doors/filter faces clear. Six small rear service-wall openings are modeled for air connections, with separate louvre faces and optional penetration/access boxes. No structural column or beam is cut. Barrier-specific dampers, protection, weathering, acoustics and inspection details remain unresolved; the model asserts no fire rating.
- Corridor visualization levels are data 3.27 m, return 3.40 m, power 3.54 m, supply 3.69 m, fire 3.84 m and refrigerant 3.92 m above each floor. Short local offsets coordinate retained sanitary extraction and the left water branch. These are deliberately packed illustrative envelopes within the current ceiling/slab gap, not approved service-zone depths. Some allowances are tight; real fittings, tolerances, insulation, supports, beams and maintenance space can require rerouting or a revised ceiling level.
- Optional insulation envelopes represent 10 mm around selected ducts and 25 mm around refrigerant placeholders solely for geometric inspection. Pipe/duct cross-sections and insulation allowances are visual assumptions, not selected sizes or thermal design. Proposed shaft sections/penetrations are coordination reservations. FR is dashed through the floor plates; new structural openings have not been approved or cut there.
- Condensate uses a schematic fall of 1 mm/m to demonstrate continuous descent; that is not a selected installation gradient. Local waste headers and ground drain candidates also fall toward their stacks/site interfaces. Traps retain their intentional upturns. Actual slopes, sizes, sewer levels, traps, air gaps, drainage protection and cleaning access need design. Buried candidates sit below floor/site surfaces and may be concealed in plan views.
- Hot/tempered branches now have local point-of-use heater/mixing reservations in service linings and at pantries. The former unsupported hot-water source at the domestic booster is removed. Heater cold connections turn around soil stacks. Duties, temperatures, safety devices and maintenance access remain unverified.
- Fire-water storage, suction arrangement, pump duties, floor-control specifications, test/drain disposal, standpipes, fire-department connection and sprinkler design remain unresolved. No sprinkler heads or coverage are implied by the dashed examples. Fire equipment remains monitoring-only; no remote control is added.
- The ground AHU occupies the clear rear area between tank and condenser reservations, with front maintenance space. Unit size/count, outdoor intake/relief separation, condenser airflow, handling and site boundaries remain unverified. Comfort ventilation, toilet extract, refrigerant cooling and possible atrium smoke control are separate systems. Smoke volumes indicate possible high/low opening zones only, with no equipment placed on the glazed roof.

## Follow-up verification — 31 October 2026

A second review identified a gap in the first clash check: it compared different service systems but excluded different circuits within the refrigerant system. Separate A/B pipes and the two condenser networks could therefore cross each other despite that check passing.

The model now applies local vertical offsets at those crossings, retaining the authored corridor footprint, source connections and same-circuit branch junctions. The east refrigerant mains begin within their own wing and use a slightly adjusted rear corridor lane. The current layout needs 36 local offsets. Every condenser-to-riser-to-indoor-unit path remains connected; the renderer uses the corrected coordinates. Separate-circuit tests include both pipe bodies and the illustrative insulation envelopes, including the pending riser reservations. These offsets do not establish refrigerant pressure-drop limits, allowable lengths, oil return, supports or manufacturer details.

Condenser pipe connections now rise beside their casings before turning across the high-level yard route. Checks include the existing modeled equipment maintenance volumes, which remain clear. Selecting one of those units restores its visibility.

The extended tests verify these corrections as well as source-to-unit graph continuity, actual rendered pipe coordinates, separate systems, retained sanitary routes and visibility behavior.
